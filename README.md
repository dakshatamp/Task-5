# Individual Case Safety Report

**Carbamazepine: Anaphylactic Reaction (Literature Case)**

## 1. ADR Identification

The case was identified during a literature search of PubMed for 2026 publications on adverse drug reactions. It is patient 14 in a case series of 15 patients with drug-related oral lesions and reactions, published as:

> Aslam S, Verma P, Rahman SZ, Ali G, Khan MH, Batool S. Pharmacovigilance through Clinical Observation: Case Series of Drug-Related Oral Lesions and Reactions. *Niger Med J*. 2026;66(5):2062-2070. PMID 42471879 (open access).

The four minimum criteria for a valid ICSR are met:

- **Identifiable reporter:** the article authors, Department of Oral Medicine and Radiology, Dr Ziauddin Ahmad Dental College and Hospital, Aligarh Muslim University, India.
- **Identifiable patient:** female, 30 years.
- **Suspect drug:** carbamazepine 400 mg.
- **Adverse reaction:** anaphylaxis, with dizziness, palpitations, loss of consciousness, urticaria, low blood pressure and tachycardia.

The case is therefore a valid ICSR. A duplicate search is to be completed before a new case is created, because the authors state that all cases in the series were reported to the Adverse Drug Reaction Monitoring Centre at JNMC, Aligarh under the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India.

## 3. Causality Assessment

The authors assessed the case as probable using the WHO-UMC scale. The company assessment used the Naranjo algorithm, scored as follows:

- Previous conclusive reports of the reaction: yes (+1)
- Event appeared after the drug was given: yes (+2)
- Improvement on withdrawal of the drug: yes (+1)
- Reappearance on re-administration: not done (0)
- Alternative causes: not reported (0)
- Placebo reaction, drug level and dose-response: not applicable (0)
- Similar reaction on previous exposure: not reported (0)
- Confirmation by objective evidence (hypotension, tachycardia, urticaria): yes (+1)

The total Naranjo score is 5, which falls in the probable category (5 to 8). The company causality assessment is therefore **probable**, in agreement with the reporter. The assessment is provisional because the time to onset and other possible causes are not documented in the article.

**Listedness:** anaphylaxis and other serious hypersensitivity reactions are listed for carbamazepine in the product information, so the event is assessed as listed (expected). This is to be confirmed against the company reference safety information in force on Day 0.

## 4. Severity Classification

**Seriousness (regulatory).** The event is classified as **serious** under the criterion "other medically significant condition". Anaphylaxis with hypotension and loss of consciousness, requiring emergency care and three weeks of oral corticosteroid treatment, is an important medical event. The article does not state hospitalisation, a life-threatening course or disability, and these points are included in the follow-up query.

**Severity (clinical).** On the modified Hartwig and Siegel scale the reaction is **Level 3, moderate**: the suspect drug was withdrawn and treatment was required, with no increase in length of stay reported. The grade will be revised if follow-up confirms hospital admission.

## 6. Case Narrative

This is a literature case reported by an oral medicine specialist in India, published in the Nigerian Medical Journal as part of a case series of 15 patients with drug-related oral lesions and reactions. This report concerns patient 14, a 30-year-old female, who experienced anaphylaxis after taking carbamazepine 400 mg. The indication, route, frequency and start date were not reported. Medical history, concomitant medications, allergy history and pregnancy status were not reported.

The patient experienced dizziness, palpitations, loss of consciousness and skin allergy. Clinical findings included low blood pressure, urticaria and tachycardia; values were not reported. The event was diagnosed as anaphylaxis. The patient received emergency care at hospital triage and was treated with oral methylprednisolone 10 mg daily for three weeks and an intravenous antihistamine (Avil 2 mL stat). Carbamazepine was withdrawn. The dechallenge was positive and the patient recovered. The authors report that patients in the series remain under biannual follow-up. Rechallenge was not reported.

The authors assessed causality as probable on the WHO-UMC scale. The company assessed the event as serious (medically significant), listed, and probably related to carbamazepine (Naranjo score 5). Follow-up information has been requested from the author.
